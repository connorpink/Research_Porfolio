This repository is where my thesis work on synthetic chest X-rays lives. Part of it became a short paper at the 39th Canadian Conference on Artificial Intelligence.

## The paper

Connor Pink and Ed Sykes. *Disentangling dataset size from synthetic diversity in tuberculosis chest X-ray classification.* Proceedings of the 39th Canadian Conference on Artificial Intelligence, PMLR 318:954–961, 2026.

<p class="flex flex-wrap gap-3">
  <a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v318/pink26a.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer" class="button-primary">PMLR page ↗</a>
  <a href="/files/Connor_s_CanAI_paper____Round_2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer" class="button-secondary">PDF ↗</a>
</p>

I compared two generators on the Tuberculosis Chest X-ray Database: a 256×256 WGAN-GP and a 512×512 latent diffusion model fine-tuned from RoentGen-v2. The diffusion model wins on image-quality metrics (lower FID and Rad-Dino FID). But when the synthetic images were used to train a DenseNet-121 classifier, neither generator beat a control that just duplicated real images up to the same count.

The best synthetic setting was diffusion at a low ratio (0.25 synthetic images per real one). Larger synthetic shares made things worse, especially with the WGAN-GP. Better-looking images don't guarantee a better classifier, and claims about synthetic data need a count-matched control.
